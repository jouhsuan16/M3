Project Process 01

Discussing the Manifesto

The manifesto did not appear as a finished text. It grew through conversation, disagreement, annotation, rewriting, and interface experiments that helped us decide what needed to stay open.

Where It Started

Before there was a manifesto, there were fragments.

Emotional numbness.

Financial pressure.

Fear of becoming adapted.

The struggle to protect freedom inside ordinary life.

We began by naming discomfort, not solutions.

Off Track started from unfinished thoughts, not finished answers.

Building a Public Form

Once the language had enough shared weight, we translated it into a website that could hold the manifesto as an experience rather than only a block of text.

We did not want the manifesto to exist only as a fixed statement or a finished document. We wanted it to remain navigable, layered, and open to re-reading.

The website allows visitors to move through the manifesto the same way we built it through fragments, tension, and multiple paths rather than a single linear explanation.

Interaction became part of meaning. Sticky notes and changing backgrounds were not decoration, they reflected how we work as a collective.

The site is not a presentation of agreement. It is a space that holds disagreement.
