Project Process 02

Building the Game Space

The game took shape by starting from familiar story logic and then quietly placing another possibility just beside it.

Where It Started

Core Idea

We design small interactive situations where players instinctively follow the "expected" solution first.

But the real possibility often exists outside that expected path.

The goal is not to win correctly, but to notice how often we obey invisible rules without questioning them.

If players begin to question the first solution that comes to mind and notice that other choices were always available, then we have achieved our purpose. If not, that is also fine—the game should still simply feel enjoyable to play.

Process Flow

Step 1

Set the Familiar Story

Start with a situation everyone already knows.

Examples

  • Shape Sorter
  • Thirsty Crow
  • Mushroom Delivery
  • Golden Egg Shell
  • Panic Tetris

The player immediately assumes there is a "correct" way.

This creates expectation.

Step 2

Encourage the Wrong Habit

Design the interface so players naturally choose the traditional solution.

Examples

  • Shape Sorter: match each shape to the bottle with the same opening.
  • Thirsty Crow: throw stones into the bottle.
  • Mushroom Delivery: walk carefully through the forest to Grandma's house.
  • Golden Egg Shell: follow the egg and choose the one that should hold the cheese.
  • Panic Tetris: stack the falling blocks neatly and clear lines.

This reveals how strongly narrative habits control decision-making.

Step 3

Hide the Simpler Possibility

The actual solution is obvious but psychologically invisible.

Examples

  • Shape Sorter: use the rock to break the bottle and make space.
  • Thirsty Crow: tilt the bottle and drink directly.
  • Mushroom Delivery: tap the phone and send the mushrooms by scooter.
  • Golden Egg Shell: drag the mouse to sniff out the cheese.
  • Panic Tetris: press the shovel button and clear the board instead.

The player can always choose differently, but usually doesn't notice first.

Step 4

Realization Moment

When the player discovers the alternative solution:

"Oh... I could just do that?"

This is the most important moment.

Step 5

Reflection Through Play

The game does not explain morality.

It simply leaves the player with a question:

"How many things in life do I solve the hard way only because I was taught that's the normal way?"

That reflection is enough.

Supporting Principle

Leave Room for the Conventional Path

We do not punish conventional thinking.

It is also okay to drop stones into the bottle for the crow, and it is also okay to play Tetris the usual way.

There is no wrong path.

Only unnoticed possibilities.

This is why the game remains playful instead of preachy.
