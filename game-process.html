Project Process 02

Building the Game Space

The game project turned our collective ideas into playful encounters. Instead of explaining everything directly, we used small games to let visitors move through pressure, curiosity, rhythm, and surprise.

Process Timeline

How the game space moved from concept to a playable set of experiments.

01

Defining the role of play

We treated play as a way of thinking, not just decoration. The games needed to extend the collective's ideas by making visitors interact, test, and respond instead of only reading.

02

Choosing mini-game directions

We developed several small formats with distinct moods and mechanics. This let the game space feel plural, playful, and slightly unpredictable rather than unified into one single tone.

03

Adjusting interaction and pacing

We tuned movement, feedback, and difficulty so the games stayed approachable while still carrying energy. The process involved repeated testing and small revisions to feel, timing, and readability.

04

Connecting the games to the whole site

The final step was making sure the game world still belonged to the collective website. We treated it as another entry point into our methods, not a disconnected extra page.

Production Notes

A summary of what the game project needed to accomplish in both concept and execution.

Design Goals

What the game space needed to do

  • Create a playful entry point into the collective without flattening its ideas.
  • Offer multiple small experiences instead of one fixed path through interaction.
  • Keep the tone lively, strange, and inviting enough for visitors to explore.

Production Work

What making it involved

  • Designing the visual language of the game landing page and each mini-game.
  • Building interactions, testing responsiveness, and adjusting moment-to-moment feedback.
  • Linking the playful experiments back to the larger website ecosystem.

Why It Matters

Play as another collective language

The games turn the site into an active encounter. They invite people to feel tempo, friction, surprise, and experimentation directly, which makes play part of the collective's public method.
